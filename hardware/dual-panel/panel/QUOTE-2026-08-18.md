## ⚠ 18 → 20 PCBA cost far more than linear scaling predicts

The quote was first taken at **18 assembled** (2 left bare, intended as passthroughs).
Going to 20 cost **+$70.45**, against a predicted +$20.

| | 18 PCBA | 20 PCBA | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Components | $170.16 | $239.28 | **+$69.12** |
| SMT assembly | $12.76 | $14.09 | +$1.33 |
| Setup / stencil / feeders / confirm / packaging | — | — | **$0.00** |
| PCB block (all of it) | $174.47 | $174.47 | **$0.00** |

**Per-board component cost ROSE with volume — $9.45 → $11.96.** That is
non-monotonic, so it is a step function somewhere across the 35 lines, not linear
cost. The obvious suspect was a WS2815 reel break; **that was checked and
disproved** — `C5446699` ships in 1000-piece packs, and both 450 (18 boards) and
500 (20 boards) round to the same single pack. **The actual cause is unidentified.**
JLC's BOM matcher shows per-line extended cost, so comparing 18 vs 20 there would
find it.

⚠ **Do not estimate a quantity change on this board by dividing and multiplying.**

**It was ordered at 20 anyway**, because `docs/BOM.md` fixes the scope at **20 panel
assemblies (2 × 9 + 2 spares)**. The 2 spares were always the plan, and the brain
is the one board that cannot be hand-built. Passthroughs cost nothing extra: the
carrier daisy-chains `RS485+/−`, `RS485_Shield`, `+12VDC` and `GND` in its own
copper (J204↔J207, J205↔J208 share nets), so **a populated carrier with the brain
unplugged IS a passthrough**. Only caveat: `LED_DATA_5V` then floats, since the
shifter U301 and R301 live on the brain — ground it at TP214 if the LEDs misbehave.
