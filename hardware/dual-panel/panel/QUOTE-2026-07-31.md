## Cost structure

Roughly **$147.58 is quantity-independent** (engineering, setup, stencil, feeder
loading, both confirm fees). The remaining ~$208.07 scales, at about **$41.61 per
panel**.

Extrapolating (linear, so a floor on the saving since components have quantity
breaks):

| qty | total | per panel |

## Panelising is confirmed working

`Setup fee $51.12` and `Stencil $16.42` are **identical to the single-board
panel-pcb quote**, and `Panel: $0.00` / `Large size: $0.00` mean JLC accepted the
customer panel with no surcharge and 228 × 143 mm didn't trip an oversize band.

That is the per-order overhead being paid **once for both boards**. Ordering them
as two designs would repeat engineering + setup + stencil + confirm-file ≈ **$94**,
plus a second shipment.

**Three lines are 83% of the increase, and none are caused by the split:**

- **ENIG** and **epoxy-filled vias** are option choices made this time and not last
  time. They would cost the same on a single-board design — the via-in-pad that
  drives the fill (RP2040 thermal array, six 0402 caps) exists on `panel-pcb` too.
- **Components +$55.75 for only 3 extra lines** is the one genuine anomaly. See
  below.

**The structural cost of splitting into two boards is about $9** — the board-area
line, and even that is mostly the rails and the empty region north/south of the
brain rather than the second board itself. Setup, stencil and engineering are
unchanged.

## Open question: the components line

$72.59 → $128.34 is +77% for +9% more BOM lines. The design's SMD content barely
changed (110 → 115 placements). Worth checking on JLC's BOM matching page, which
itemises per-line cost. Likely causes, in order of suspicion:

1. **The old quote under-counted.** Manually-picked rows must have their checkbox
   ticked — rows left "to be confirmed" are silently *not* assembled, and so not
   priced. If `panel-pcb` had unconfirmed rows, $72.59 covered fewer parts.
2. **Different part matching.** JLC's auto-matcher picks different parts run to run,
   and it favours dead listings; the two quotes may not be buying the same parts.
3. Known big-ticket items to check first: the **THVD1429** (the 1419 was ~$5/pc, so
   ~$25 across 5 panels) and the **125 WS2815s** (25 per panel × 5).

## Notes for the next quote

- ENIG is deliberate — a 0.4 mm-pitch QFN-56 plus filled-and-capped vias is exactly
  where a flat finish earns its keep.
- The via-covering lever, if ever wanted: move the six 0402 vias (C5, C6, C7, C9,
  C11, C15) out of their pads, leaving only the RP2040 thermal array, and a cheaper
  via option becomes viable. That is a design change, and $19 on a $356 order.
- Baking came in flat at $7.88, not per-part.
