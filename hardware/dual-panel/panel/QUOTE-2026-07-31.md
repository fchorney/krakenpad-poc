## ⚠ Panel format: use Column = 1, Row = 1

**This is the expensive gotcha.** On "panel by customer", JLC asks for column and
row counts. Entering **2 × 1** — geometrically true, two boards side by side —
makes their system read qty 5 panels as **10 assembled boards**, and it prices
components and placement for twice the units:

| line | Column = 2 | Column = 1 | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Components | $128.34 | $73.32 | 1.75× |
| SMT assembly | $7.56 | $4.31 | 1.75× |

That is **$58 of phantom assembly**, and nothing else on the quote moves.

**How it was diagnosed, since the same trap will look different next time:** the
two lines that changed are the only two that scale with *unit count*. Everything
that scales with *complexity* — feeders loading ($53.55), setup ($51.12), stencil
($16.42) — was identical to the cent. Same reels, same setup, same number of
sides, but 1.75× the parts, means more units are being built, not more work per
unit. Both ratios matching to three decimals (1.744 / 1.742) confirmed it.

Use **1 × 1** and describe the panel in the order remarks instead:

> Customer panel: 2 different designs, 1× carrier (139.2 × 127.0 mm) + 1× brain
> (70.9 × 62.5 mm), joined by mouse-bite tabs to a 5 mm rail frame.

## Panelising is confirmed working

`Setup fee $51.12` and `Stencil $16.42` are **identical to the single-board quote**,
and `Panel: $0.00` / `Large size: $0.00` mean the customer panel was accepted with
no surcharge and 228 × 143 mm didn't trip an oversize band.

That per-order overhead is paid **once for both boards**. Two separate designs
would repeat engineering + setup + stencil + confirm-file ≈ **$94**, plus a second
shipment — which is what the one-project, one-panel structure exists to avoid.

## Notes

- **ENIG is deliberate** — a 0.4 mm-pitch QFN-56 plus filled-and-capped vias is
  exactly where a flat finish earns its keep.
- **Via covering ($19.09)** is the price of via-in-pad. The lever, if ever wanted:
  move the six 0402 vias (C5, C6, C7, C9, C11, C15) out of their pads, leaving only
  the RP2040 thermal array, and a cheaper option becomes viable. Design change, and
  it's 6% of the order.
- **Baking came in flat at $7.88**, not per-part.
- The 2026-07-18 panel-pcb quote's $72.59 components line was **not** under-counting
  as first suspected — $73.58 here confirms it was right all along.
